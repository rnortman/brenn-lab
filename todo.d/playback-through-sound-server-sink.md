A playback role can be configured for a sink of the desktop's sound server (`output = "pulse"`), and holds the sink's volume and mute under its cap. It has played on one sink, an LG-PL5 Bluetooth speaker over A2DP on PipeWire's pulse server, in the audible bench, and `docs/reference/experiments.md`, *Measured on a Bluetooth speaker*, states what that measured: the play found in the capture, the stream at 0 dB and unmoved, the sink read back at the level asked and restored, a play over the cap refused with nothing heard, the server's latency estimate against where alignment found the play, a 16 kHz one-channel source on a 48 kHz two-channel sink, and healthy ends of play. Everything else is held only against a stand-in sound server this tree wrote, from the same reading of the protocol as the client.

Unmeasured on a real server: whether anything sounds on another sink during a play; what the server does with a channel that has no place on the sink, and which of the sink's channels a one-channel stream sounds on; that a corked stream leaves a suspended sink silent (so a dry run on a sink is made only inside a window); whether a base volume under full marks the mixer's 0 dB (the one sink played on has base volume 0 mB); what the server does with frames handed over after an underrun; what the server sends a stream with no pre-buffer when its buffer plays out at the end of a healthy play, and what offset an underflow message carries then and at an underrun; what the server tells a stream whose sink is suspended while it plays; whether a sink's latency figure can grow by seconds while a buffer plays out, which would fail a healthy play at the end-of-play bound (it did not on the one sink played on); and the speaker's fade-in after minutes of silence, which no play gave a chance to show, since every sounding play came seconds after another stream on the speaker.

A sound at the sink's silent minimum, read from eleven kept ramps: with the sink at −28899 mB, every play of the ramp's first step, primer and marked play alike, sounded a burst of about half a second at its start. It began 50 to 100 ms before where the play's first frame sounds, peaked at −45 dB in the 500–3500 Hz band on the Flex's raw microphones (the primer at the detected level, −8400 mB, read about −53 dB there) and decayed into the room; its peak varied by under 2.5 dB over two hours. The maintainer's account of it: it is the speaker's own chirp, the sound this speaker makes when its volume is set above or below a limit. That agrees with what the runs ruled out, with the figures in `docs/reference/experiments.md`, *Measured on a Bluetooth speaker*: it is not the played content, not a play's tail at the restored volume, and it does not follow the level asked (no step from −12000 to −9000 mB sounds it). The runs did not show the cause themselves. The bench's ramp still makes its first step at the sink's minimum, so a ramp on this speaker sounds the chirp on that step and on no other; nothing is open about it.

The desktop's session manager (WirePlumber) still holds a saved stream state under the client's name, `brenn-lab pulse-client`, left by plays made before the client opted out of restore; it was not removed, since removing it changes the desktop's sound state. The client sets `state.restore-props` and `state.restore-target` to `false` on every stream, and the stand-in holds only that it sends them. On the real server, the saved state, one channel, was not rewritten by the two-channel plays made after the opt-out, so the session manager stopped remembering the client's streams. Whether it also stopped restoring onto them is not seen, since the client sets each stream to 0 dB once made, which covers a restored volume either way. No play was made with a remembered sink other than the one named, so whether `state.restore-target` keeps a stream on its named sink is not seen either.

Until the server's end-of-play messages are measured, an underrun as a play's last write is sent is not on the play's record: the play ends `completed` with no underrun and its markers are assessed `expected`, though frames of the last write may not have sounded. Done for that part = such an underrun is on the record and a test holds it.

The work's audible tests need a Bluetooth speaker that is already connected. The work connects none, and changes nothing else of the desktop's sound or Bluetooth state.

Done when a playback role on a sound-server sink has played under its cap in an agreed window with an audible-lane test that holds each unmeasured item above, and the reference documents state what was measured.
