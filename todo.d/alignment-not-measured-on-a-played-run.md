The harness lines up a played source with a capture by finding the source's two markers, and its tests hold that on synthetic signals and, for false alarms only, on ambient sound in the hardware lane. It has not been run on a marked source played through a real output into a real room and recorded by a real device. So the marker's fitness there, the found/not-found threshold of 0.25, the ±500 ppm it tolerates inside one marker, and the drift it reports for one device playing and recording on one clock (which should be none) are unmeasured. Marked plays at once on several outputs, and the rule for how many may overlap, are held on synthetic signals only, and cannot be measured until the harness has a second kind of output. The audible-lane test still finds its clips with test-support code of its own. The run's assessment has been read on recordings of two earlier played runs, made before plays recorded their markers: levels, samples at the format's limit, loss and the play side. Those runs carry no marker record, so the assessment's markers and drift on a played run are unmeasured as well.

The work: the audible-lane test plays a marked source on the Flex, aligns the run with `lab align`, and asserts the report: both markers found on the raw microphone channels, the offset within the clock tie's stated bound, no drift beyond a stated tolerance, and the same answer on a second run. The same test asserts the run's assessment on that marked played run: `answer` `clean` with nothing in `not_assessed`, the play located with the drift alignment states, and the played source's level on the raw microphone channels reported. That run of the audible lane is also the first time the playback role's reporting of a play whose end could not be reported meets a device, and the lane's existing assertions on each play's record must hold. The test-support search is removed if nothing else needs it.

Done = that test has passed in a window the maintainer agreed, and the reference document states what was measured.
