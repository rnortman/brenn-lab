In brenn-lab. The maintainer asked that a test on a speaker that fades in after silence start with a tone and a wait of a few seconds, with the sink held open through the wait. The audible bench and `examples/level_step.py` instead play the lead-in as a play of its own: its stream is closed and the measured play opens a new one about a second later, because a playback role makes and closes one stream per play; a play with a stated start holds its stream open, unwritten, from its readying to its start, and the lead-in is still another play's stream. No fade-in was seen in the runs of 2026-11-14, each of which came seconds after another stream on the speaker, so whether a fade-in returns across that gap is not measured. The work: a way for a playback role on a sound-server sink to keep its output open from a lead-in to the source that follows it (one stream across both, or a lead-in the role plays ahead of a source on the same stream), with what that changes in a play's marks and kept copy stated in the run-directory reference. Done when the bench's lead-in and its measured play share one open stream and a test against the stand-in sound server holds that the stream is not closed between them, or when a run made after minutes of silence shows the fade-in ended inside the lead-in with the gap as it is and the experiments reference says so.
