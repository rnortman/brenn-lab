The run-directory reference says that on a device that samples playback and capture on one clock the offset between a play's kept copy and the capture is constant across a play with no underrun. On one run of the reSpeaker Flex playing speech through its own output while recording, it was not. Analysed afterwards by generalised cross-correlation in quarter-second and half-second windows, the delay of the recorded echo behind the kept copy held still for seconds and then stepped, within single plays of about 19 s, most often by about −8 frames and about +24 frames at 16 kHz, up to nine times in a play and by up to 36 frames in all, on all four raw microphones together, with no underrun and no capture gap reported. One play in 25 had no step. The markers say the capture is what moves: in the same run a second source, played through a different sound card and marked the same way, shows a net shift between its start and end markers of −22 to +15 frames that follows the device's own play by play (correlation 0.9 over 25 plays), while in two runs of that second source with nothing played through the device its net shift stays within −4 to +8 frames and repeats play for play. So a measured ratio a few tens of parts per million from 1 on such a device may be the net of steps in the capture and not a rate. The windowed analysis is one analysis of one run and has not been repeated. Not known: where in the capture path the steps arise (the device, the USB stream, the recorder); whether sound is lost or repeated at a step; whether they occur on other devices that play and record at once. Done when a run made to answer it says where the steps arise and what happens to the sound at one, the harness prevents them or reports each one in the run directory, and the reference states what was found in place of the constant-offset sentence; or an entry says what is left.
