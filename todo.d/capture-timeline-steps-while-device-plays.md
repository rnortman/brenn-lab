The run-directory reference says that on a device that samples playback and capture on one clock the offset between a play's kept copy and the capture is constant across a play with no underrun. On the reSpeaker Flex (XVF3800, six-channel USB audio image) playing through its own output while recording, it is not: the delay of the recorded echo behind the kept copy steps within single plays, in 11 to 17 of every 20 plays of 20 s. Found, in runs made to answer it with one process directly on ALSA and none of the harness's software: the steps arise inside the device, on both sides of it, whenever it plays and records together, and not once in 7.7 minutes of capture alone. The host's streams are nominal: capture and playback positions follow host time to within half a frame, with no xrun. Capture side, two signatures. First, a run of exactly 24 frames in which all six channels are zero, after which the content is either 24 frames later (nothing lost, 24 frames of silence inserted) or 16 to 17 frames earlier (40 frames of content lost behind the zeros), the frame count unmoved. Second, rarely, one USB packet of 8 frames that never reaches ALSA, seen as a jump of −8 in the frame count against host time, with 8 frames of content lost. Playback side, the most frequent: 8 played frames dropped, or 1 to 7 extra frames played, after the USB OUT packets; the host's playback position, ALSA's delay, the capture count and the captured data show nothing, and only the echo locates them. The offset stays within about −17 to +18 frames and does not accumulate. Not removed by one process in place of two, continuous playback, opening playback first, periods of 64 or 320 frames, a louder level, or leaving no silence between plays. An earlier reading in this entry, that the markers show the capture is what moves, is overturned in part: most steps are on the playback side. Both capture-side signatures pass today as clean audio, with zero underruns and zero capture gaps reported. In later runs with the chip's own echo reference routed to a capture channel (brenn-pod's XVF3800 control-protocol reference, section 9, says how), 45 zero runs fell in six runs of 20 plays. Each of the 43 inside a play zeroed the reference channel over the same frames as the microphones and shifted neither against the other; the other 2 fell in the silence between plays, where the reference is zero anyway and no shift was measured. At 40 of the 43 the content after the zeros was 24 frames later, and at the other three 15, 16 and 26 frames earlier. Not known: what the USB packets carry at each event (`bench-privileged-usb-trace`); whether the device used only for capture is free of content slips that leave no trace in the stream; why a zero run is followed by +24 in most cases and −16 in some; whether other devices that play and record at once do the same. Done when the harness reports, as capture events in the run directory, each run of 24 or more all-zero frames on every channel, to the frame, and each one-packet deficit of the frame count against the frame log's host times, to about a second; and the reference states, in place of the constant-offset sentence, that this device's offset is not constant while it plays and what a reader must do about it; or an entry says what is left.
