Every marker is written at a fixed peak of half full scale (`AMPLITUDE` in `brenn_lab/marker.py`), whatever the level of the content it surrounds. Where the content is much quieter than that, as speech at −26 dBFS RMS is, the marker is by far the loudest thing in the play, and a capture channel that carries gain of its own reaches the format's limit on the markers before the content is as loud as the experiment wants. The run is then assessed with `channel_at_limit`, and the experiment has to play everything lower. An experiment recording speech has had to play it below the level it wanted for this reason. The level is not only `mark`'s: `recognise` regenerates both markers at `AMPLITUDE` and refuses a file whose samples are more than one least significant bit away, so a marker written at, or scaled to, another level is not recognised as one. Done when an experiment can have a marker written at a level it chooses, a file marked that way is recognised and its plays are aligned as now, the reference documents say how the level is chosen and how low it can go before alignment loses the marker, and a test holds each.
